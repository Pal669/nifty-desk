Var 1 · FII vs Retail positioning

Nifty index futures only
What it measures
  • Net FII = FII long contracts − FII short contracts (Nifty index futures).
  • Net Client = Retail long − Retail short (same instrument).
  • Extent of Diff = the size of the gap between Net Client and Net FII. A big gap means positioning is crowded and one-sided. A small gap means the two are tangled together.
  • Stock futures are left out on purpose. Source: NSE participant-wise open interest file, every evening.
The cut-off ladder (5-year sheet, Sep 2021 – Sep 2026, 1,235 trading days)
PercentileExtent of DiffWhat it means

Example: a reading of 5.24 is above 3.95, so it counts as extreme. It sits between p95 and p99, which means only about 4–5% of days in five years were more crowded than that. A percentile (simple: the share of past days that were lower) of 90 means 90% of days were below this level.
These cut-offs are fixed numbers from Sep 2026. They are not recalculated as new days are added.
Rule 1 · Crowding = fade the crowd Extent of Diff ≥ 3.95
The rule. When the gap is extreme and FII is net SHORT, Nifty tends not to fall from there. It stays flat or grinds up.
Why (your logic). "Everybody knows FII is short." When the whole market can see one side is crowded, that move is already priced in. So the opposite of the obvious move plays out.
Mirror. Extreme gap + FII net LONG → Nifty tends not to rise.
Evidence — short side (contrarian long trade, 5Y sheet)
Hold forSignalsRightSuccess rateAvg gain when rightAvg loss when wrong
10 days885967.0%+2.8%−1.7%
20 days856374.1%+3.3%−1.8%
1 month856070.6%+3.5%−1.8%

  • Best hold: 20 days. Highest hit rate, and the average gain is nearly twice the average loss.
  • Do not hold for 2 months. It becomes a coin flip and losses nearly double. The edge is a short squeeze, not a long trend.
  • Honest sample size: the signal days come in clusters. They are really about 8–10 separate episodes, not 85 independent bets. So read 74% as "worked in about 7 of 9 real episodes".
  • Long side: only 2 episodes in five years (both in 2024). One worked perfectly and one fired too early while FII was still building. That is not tradeable on its own. Use Rule 1C to time it.
Rule 1B · Near zero = wait, then follow FII Extent of Diff ≤ 0.70
The rule. When the gap is near zero, nobody has a clear edge and a crossover may be coming. Do not take a stance straight away. Watch for about 5 trading days to see which way FII builds its position. Then trade in the same direction as FII.
Why. This is the opposite of Rule 1. Crowded and extreme = fade it. Flat and uncertain = wait and follow whoever commits first.
Evidence (5Y sheet).
  • FII built long → Nifty +1.04% / +1.41% / +1.72% over 5 / 10 / 20 days.
  • FII built short → Nifty −0.69% / −0.70% / −0.77%.
  • A normal day averages only +0.13% / +0.27% / +0.55%.
  • This is the strongest statistical result in the model. The odds of it being pure chance are below 1 in 100 billion at every horizon. It still holds after grouping into 40 build-long and 53 build-short separate episodes.
Rule 1C · Early warning: a fall is being loaded Watch, not a trigger
Switch it on when all three are true:
  1. FII is net long,
  2. FII has kept adding to that long for several weeks,
  3. and Nifty has been rising over the same period.
Why. A big FII long has to be closed one day, and the only way to close it is to sell. That selling is the fall. You cannot time it to the day, but you can see it being loaded.
Key fact. The build is slow and the exit is fast. In the one real example (19 Jun – 4 Oct 2024), FII built for about 3.5 months to a five-year record of +392. Then it unwound in about 5 trading days (+338 → +318 → +163 → +83), and Nifty fell from about 26,200.
Confidence: 1 episode only. Treat it as pattern recognition, not proof.

Var 2 · Nifty futures open interest

All 3 expiries added together
What it measures
Open interest (OI) (simple: the number of futures contracts still open) across the near, mid and far month, plus how much it changed today. It is paired with Nifty's move for the day.
The 4 regimes
OINiftyRegimeWhat is happening
UpUpLong BuildupFresh buyers entering
UpDownShort BuildupFresh sellers entering
DownUpShort CoveringSellers exiting (buying back)
DownDownLong UnwindingBuyers exiting (selling)
The first two are your own rule. The last two use the standard F&O names, which you approved. Right now this is a labelling rule (which regime are we in). It is not yet a prediction rule, because no forward-return backtest has been run.
Speed matters, not just the label
  • Rate of OI change = today's change in OI ÷ yesterday's OI × 100.
  • Streak = how many days in a row the same regime has held, the average rate over the streak, and whether it is accelerating or slowing.
Unusual volume ("a huge day")
A day is unusual when today's change in OI, ignoring direction, is bigger than the average of the last 5 days. This is relative to recent days, not a fixed contract count.
Key levels from huge days
Day typeLevel recordedBecomesYour logic
Long Unwinding + unusual volumeNear-month futures LOWSupport (floor)A big block of longs was forced out there. The market should not fall much below it unless FII builds fresh shorts.
Short Covering + unusual volumeNear-month futures HIGHResistance (ceiling)A big block of shorts was covered there. The market should not rise much above it unless FII builds fresh longs.
A level stays active until a newer qualifying day replaces it.
